## Preferences

- **Simple, organized screens.** Make the main task and next action easy to understand. Prefer useful information over decoration; minimal does not mean empty or under-informative.
- **Restrained branding in working tools.** Keep a supplied logo and product name visible without oversized marks or unnecessarily bold titles. Let the task lead the hierarchy. Public-facing or brand-focused pages may need a different emphasis. Use an appropriate supplied logo asset for the favicon rather than an invented initial or generic icon.
- **Little repetition.** Give each fact a useful home. Repeat only when it helps the current decision; do not fill multiple side panels with the same background information.
- **Help that is easy to find.** Put occasional rules, explanations and useful links behind clearly named, keyboard-accessible accordions or panels. Keep relevant blockers, errors, amounts due and important consequences visible when needed. Essential help must not depend on hover.
- **Summaries that help verification.** Use supporting space to show entered information and meaningful status, with edit actions where permitted. Avoid static filler that competes with the current task.
- **Visible progress and a clear exit.** In multi-step or multi-person work, show where the user is, whose information is active and what remains. Let users revisit permitted steps without losing valid work. Make back/exit and saved-versus-unsaved effects understandable.
- **Consistent interactions.** Reuse labels, controls, status meanings, typography and spacing. Organize tables around decisions people compare; place secondary facts near their related primary information and deeper audit detail in the record view.
- **8-point layout spacing by default.** For a new design without an approved system, use 8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48 and 64 for layout and grouping. Small optical adjustments may use smaller increments deliberately. Preserve an existing approved spacing system, including a 4px system, unless the user asks to change it. The spacing scale is not a mandatory font-size scale.

## Scope

Apply these preferences to the actual audience and screen job. They do not require every product to have a wizard, sidebar, summary panel, table or logo where none exists. Add no workflow merely to demonstrate a preference.

Event eligibility, ages, guardians, waivers, fees, payment policy, COC, approval authority and data access are product rules. They are not inherited from this profile. General edge-case reasoning belongs in ShipRight's core skills; project rules remain in the project's brief.

When producing a design handoff, identify this profile as the selected preference source and record any project-specific departure briefly. Do not reopen settled choices or add a separate approval ceremony for applying these defaults.
